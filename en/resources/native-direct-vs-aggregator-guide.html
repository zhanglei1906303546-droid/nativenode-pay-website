In this context, payment, as the "last mile" of the business loop, has risen to a strategic height. We have observed a clear industry turning point: traditional "Aggregator Gateways" are losing their dominance, replaced by "Native Direct Gateways" with better performance and more transparent data. Chapter 1: The "Invisible Ceiling" of Traditional Aggregators

Over the past decade, aggregator gateways have won the market with the convenience of their "one-stop integration." However, in the ultra-high concurrency and extremely refined risk control environment of 2026, their disadvantages are becoming increasingly apparent:

  • 1
    **Conversion Loss Due to Redundant Links**: The traditional aggregation model typically involves multiple layers of gateway hops. For every intermediate hop added, the payment success rate (Approval Rate) drops by an average of 2%-5%. In mobile impulsive consumption scenarios, the loading delay caused by redirects is the primary cause of checkout abandonment.
  • 2
    **The "Black Box" State of Data**: Since aggregators process transactions secondarily, they often cannot transmit the full 3DS 2.0 data or the original context required by the issuing bank. This leads to a high rate of false positives in risk control, with many legitimate large orders being blocked by the banking system as high-risk transactions.
  • 3
    **Lagging Settlement Cycles**: Multiple layers of agent nesting mean a long chain for capital recovery. Settlement cycles of T+7 or even longer severely limit the cash flow flexibility of global enterprises for rapid UA (User Acquisition) advertising in 2026.
